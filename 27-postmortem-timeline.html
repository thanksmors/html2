The webhooks that quietly stopped

For five and a half hours on June 24, roughly one in five outbound webhooks was accepted, acknowledged, and then dropped on the floor — with a 2xx to the caller and not a single alert. This is the record of how we found out (a customer told us), what it took to stop it, and what we're changing so a silence like this can never pass for health again.

What happened

A routine deploy at 08:51 shipped a refactor of the delivery worker's retry queue. The refactor renamed the dead-letter routing key; the consumer that re-enqueues failed deliveries kept listening on the old name. Failed webhooks — about 19% of volume, thanks to an unrelated TLS change at a large customer — were acknowledged off the main queue, published to a routing key with zero consumers, and silently discarded by the broker.

Every dashboard stayed green. Delivery attempts were up, error rates were flat, and the dead-letter depth gauge read zero — which we had always read as "nothing is failing" when it also, it turns out, means "failures aren't arriving." The gap was invisible to us and perfectly visible to customer Brightline, whose order-sync integration went quiet at breakfast.

Contributing factors

Check items off as their fixes land — the list is the working state of the cleanup, and it saves with the page.

  • Dead-letter routing key was renamed in the publisher without a matching change to the consumer binding — the two live in different repos with no shared contract test. process
  • Broker silently discards publishes to unroutable keys; mandatory-flag returns were never enabled on the delivery exchange. config
  • No end-to-end delivery SLO — we alert on error rates, and a dropped message produces no error anywhere. observability
  • Dead-letter queue depth of zero was read as healthy; it is equally consistent with "the pipe to the queue is severed." observability
  • Canary stage exercises the happy path only — no synthetic failing webhook flows through retry and dead-letter during rollout. deploy
  • First detection came from a customer 46 minutes in; support→on-call escalation added another 12 before an engineer looked. detection

Five whys

From the symptom down to the thing we actually have to fix.

  1. Why did customers stop receiving webhooks?

    Failed deliveries were re-published to a routing key with no bound queue, so the broker discarded them.

  2. Why was the routing key unbound?

    The publisher rename shipped without the consumer-side binding change — they live in different repos and nothing ties them together.

  3. Why did no test or rollout stage catch it?

    The contract between publisher and consumer is implicit; the canary only sends successful deliveries, so the dead-letter path was never exercised.

  4. Why didn't monitoring notice 41,000 missing messages?

    Every alert we own measures errors and latency. A cleanly discarded message produces neither — absence has no metric.

  5. Why does absence have no metric?

    We never defined an end-to-end delivery SLO: accepted events versus delivered-or-parked events, reconciled continuously. That's the fix everything else hangs off.
